Is your family member eligible to apply for bail?

BailSetu checks whether a person in jail may be able to apply for bail under a law called Section 479. The law says: if a person has spent a long time in jail while waiting for trial, they may be released on bail. Enter the undertrial ID and your family access code below.

Demo access code for all sample records: DEMO-2026 · Try UT-001, UT-002 or UT-004 to see the different outcomes. Legal documents are in English.

Your information stays private

Records are never listed publicly. In deployment, each family gets a unique access code from the jail or the DLSA, and a family sees only its own family member's record. Without the correct code, nothing is shown.

Need free legal help?

Every district has a District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) that gives free legal aid to people who cannot afford a lawyer, including undertrials. Ask the jail authorities or the DLSA office in your district. BailSetu does not replace a lawyer — it helps the lawyer and the family see the position clearly.

The law already says these people must be released on bail after a fixed time in custody (Section 479). But nobody tracks custody time systematically — so people stay in jail for years after the law says they can apply for bail. BailSetu fixes the tracking, not the law.

      Legal basis & safeguards
      
      
      Section 479, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023: an undertrial must be released on bail after detention of one-half of the maximum sentence for the offence — one-third for first-time offenders. S.479(2): no release under S.479 where multiple cases are pending. S.479(3): the jail superintendent must apply to the court when the threshold is crossed. The provisos to S.479(1) let the court, after hearing the Public Prosecutor and recording reasons, order continued detention — release is not automatic. The Explanation to S.479 excludes delay caused by the accused from the custody period. S.479 does not apply to offences punishable with death or life imprisonment. The Supreme Court (23 Aug 2024) held S.479 applies retrospectively, with steps to be taken as expeditiously as possible and preferably within three months.
